# FUL.HOUSE — Product Vision

## One-line idea

**You already built a life online. FUL.HOUSE gives it a home.**

FUL.HOUSE is a living digital house generated from a person's real digital identity, activity, work, interests, content and connected services.

It is not a profile page, not a Linktree clone and not a decorative metaverse house. The house is a visual, interactive representation of the owner's digital life and can continue to work for the owner while they are away.

---

## Core product thesis

A person already has a fragmented digital identity spread across LinkedIn, Instagram, GitHub, Spotify, YouTube, X, Threads, calendars, portfolios, stores, wallets and other services.

FUL.HOUSE connects those sources, analyzes them with AI and turns them into a personalized interactive home.

The user should not manually build the house from scratch.

**The house is discovered from the user.**

The more of their digital life they connect, the more the house learns about them and physically evolves.

---

## Core experience

A new user arrives at an almost empty house.

An AI butler welcomes them:

> Welcome home. This place doesn't know you yet.

The butler offers connected sources. Each connection reveals new aspects of the user's identity and can unlock rooms, furniture, objects, memories, services or behaviors.

The user should feel, after several minutes:

> This is actually my house.

That emotional recognition is the primary onboarding goal.

---

# 1. AI Butler

The AI Butler is the main interface of FUL.HOUSE.

It is not a chatbot widget floating over the UI. It is a character that belongs to the house and understands the owner, the rooms, the connected services, visitors and commerce.

### Butler responsibilities

- Welcome the owner home.
- Explain what changed while the owner was away.
- Help connect new services.
- Explain why rooms or objects were created.
- Answer visitor questions about the owner using only information the owner has authorized.
- Guide visitors to projects, products, services, galleries and contact methods.
- Manage visitors and messages.
- Surface sales and earned VIRT.
- Suggest improvements to the house.
- Help organize imported content.
- Act as a sales and representation agent when allowed.

### Example owner return

> Welcome back. While you were away, 14 people visited your office. Your new GitHub project has been added to the workshop. Someone purchased your contract template for 300 VIRT. Your latest trip has been added to the travel shelf. Also, the cat destroyed a plant.

### Example visitor interaction

Visitor:

> What does Stan work on?

Butler:

> Stan builds software products and currently has three active projects. I can show you his workshop.

Visitor:

> Does he offer consulting?

Butler:

> Yes. He currently offers this service for 1,500 VIRT. I can show you the details.

### Long-term idea

The Butler gradually becomes a permissioned digital representative of the owner.

**Your house works while you're away.**

# 3. AI generates the house from the person

The architecture should not be a rigid mapping where every LinkedIn connection always creates the same office and every Instagram connection always creates the same gallery.

Sources provide **signals**, not blueprints.

The AI builds an identity model based on authorized data such as:

- Profession
- Work
- Projects
- Skills
- Hobbies
- Aesthetic preferences
- Music
- Travel
- Creative output
- Interests
- Public content
- Products/services

That identity model influences:

- Architecture
- Rooms
- Furniture
- Decoration
- Lighting
- Objects
- Suggested pets
- Layout
- Style

Two people with the same profession should be able to receive completely different homes.

### Example

LinkedIn: architect

Instagram: mostly brutalist architecture and travel

Spotify: jazz

YouTube: design / architecture

FUL.HOUSE may infer:

- Architect
- Strong interest in brutalism
- Frequent traveler
- Contemporary design preference
- Jazz listener

The generated house can reflect those characteristics.

# 4. House as digital identity

The house should communicate who the owner is without requiring visitors to read a conventional profile or résumé.

Visitors should understand the owner by exploring.

Examples:

- Projects on the desk.
- Certificates on the wall.
- Books representing knowledge or interests.
- Photos in albums.
- A workshop showing active work.
- Reviews or achievements represented as objects.
- A music room reflecting taste.
- A gallery reflecting creative output.

The product should prefer **showing identity spatially** over displaying endless lists and cards.

---

# 5. House aging, dust and absence

The house must feel alive.

If the owner is active, the house feels occupied.

If the owner disappears for a long time, subtle environmental effects appear.

Examples:

- Dust accumulates.
- Plants begin to wilt.
- Mailbox fills.
- Lights are off.
- Rooms look less maintained.

The effect must feel charming rather than punitive.

### Return experience

When the owner returns:

- Butler welcomes them.
- Windows/opening animation.
- Cleaning begins.
- Pet reacts.
- The house becomes active again.

The goal is emotional retention rather than a conventional streak counter.

# 9. VIRT economy

Working name for the internal currency: **VIRT**.

The initial version should be platform credits rather than a publicly tradable crypto token.

Reasons:

- Avoid unnecessary speculation.
- Avoid making the economy dependent on token price.
- Reduce regulatory complexity at the earliest stage.
- Keep the product focused on utility.
- Validate whether the internal economy works before considering deeper financialization.

### Basic economic loop

**Create → Sell → Earn VIRT → Improve House → Attract Visitors → Sell More → Grow House**

### VIRT can be spent on

- House expansion
- New rooms
- Floors
- Furniture
- Gardens
- Pets
- Pet accessories
- AI Butler upgrades
- Visual themes
- Special objects
- Other people's products/services
- Professional services inside the marketplace

### Important principle

**Money builds the house.**

Instead of wealth being only a balance number, legitimate economic activity can visibly change the owner's space.

A person's history of creating value can become architecture.

# 14. Privacy model

FUL.HOUSE depends on trust.

Core rules:

1. Connecting a service does not mean making everything public.
2. Private data remains private unless explicitly published.
3. AI may use permitted private information to personalize the owner's experience without exposing it to visitors.
4. Visitors only receive information that the owner allows the Butler or house to reveal.
5. Sensitive sources require clear permission boundaries.
6. Imported content should be removable and disconnectable.
7. The owner should understand why an object or room exists and which source created it.

Every generated object should eventually support a provenance view such as:

> Created from: LinkedIn / Instagram / GitHub / Manual

# 15. The living house

The house should change over time as the person's real digital life changes.

Examples:

- New GitHub repository → new project in workshop.
- New role on LinkedIn → office changes.
- New trip on Instagram → travel album appears.
- New product → object appears for sale.
- New achievements → physical trophies/certificates.
- Long inactivity → dust and quiet house.
- Return → house wakes up.

The product therefore becomes something the user can revisit rather than a profile they configure once and forget.

# 16. Digital twin direction

Long-term, FUL.HOUSE can become a user-controlled digital twin built from connected sources.

Not an avatar clone.

The system understands, with user permission:

- What the owner does
- What they create
- What they know
- Their interests
- Their active projects
- Public work history
- Products/services they sell
- Their selected tastes and preferences

The house is the visual interface to that digital twin.

The Butler is the conversational interface.

# 17. Core loops

## Identity loop

Connect source → AI learns → room/object appears → house becomes more personal → connect another source.

## Activity loop

Real-world digital activity → connected source changes → house changes → user returns to see what happened.

## Social loop

Build house → share `ful.house/name` → visitors explore → interact/buy/contact → owner receives activity → house becomes more valuable.

## Economy loop

Create real work → list it in house → visitor buys using VIRT → owner earns VIRT → owner upgrades house or buys another user's work.

## Retention loop

Time passes → house changes / dust / messages / pet / new synced activity → Butler summarizes changes → user returns.

# 18. MVP recommendation

The MVP should prove one thing:

**Can FUL.HOUSE generate a place that immediately feels like the user?**

Avoid building a full metaverse, marketplace and city at once.

### MVP Phase 1

1. Account creation.
2. Claim username: `ful.house/name`.
3. Empty starter room/house.
4. AI Butler onboarding.
5. Connect 2-3 initial data sources.
6. AI analyzes authorized source data.
7. Generate a personalized 2.5D/isometric house.
8. Generate rooms/objects from source signals.
9. Owner can choose public/private per generated item.
10. Public visitor mode.
11. Visitor can ask the Butler basic questions about public owner data.
12. Share public house link.

### Best initial integrations

Potential first three:

- LinkedIn / professional profile source
- Instagram or a user-authorized photo source
- GitHub

Actual API feasibility must be validated before implementation because platform access policies can restrict what data can be imported.

### Visual technology

Start with lightweight 2.5D/isometric scenes or layered interactive rooms.

Do not start with a heavy open-world 3D engine.

Requirements:

- Fast on mobile
- Shareable
- Smooth entrance animation
- Clickable objects
- Easy for AI to compose
- Low device load

The phone should remain a phone, not become a pocket heater.

# 23. Product architecture concept

Core entities:

### User
Identity, permissions, username, connected accounts.

### House
Generated visual home, state, progression and public/private configuration.

### Room
Semantic area representing part of the owner's identity or activity.

### Object
Functional or decorative item linked to source data, content, work, service or achievement.

### Source
Connected external service or manually provided data.

### Identity Signal
AI-generated structured interpretation of source data.

### Butler
AI agent with permissioned access to house knowledge and visitor/owner tools.

### Pet
Persistent digital companion with state and behaviors.

### Visitor
Authenticated or anonymous user interacting with a public house.

### Product / Service
Real digital output or service offered by an owner.

### VIRT Ledger
Internal credits earned/spent inside FUL.HOUSE.

### Event
Activity that changes the house: source sync, visit, sale, new project, absence, return, etc.

# 24. Suggested AI pipeline

1. Connect source.
2. Import only authorized data.
3. Normalize source data.
4. Extract identity signals.
5. Classify signals by confidence and sensitivity.
6. Suggest rooms / objects / style changes.
7. Ask for owner approval where appropriate.
8. Generate/update house manifest.
9. Render changes.
10. Update Butler knowledge base with public/private boundaries.

The renderer should consume a structured **House Manifest**, not raw LLM prose.

Example conceptual structure:

```json
{
  "owner": "stan",
  "style": ["brutalist", "dark", "futuristic"],
  "rooms": [
    {
      "type": "workshop",
      "source": "github",
      "objects": [
        {
          "type": "project_terminal",
          "title": "Project Name",
          "visibility": "public"
        }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

This keeps AI interpretation separate from deterministic rendering.
